# Where We Go From Here — design discovery

This is a working record for a design interview, not a finalized game design document. The core concept includes confirmed interview decisions. Original examples remain provisional. Recommendations are explicitly labeled and unanswered questions remain undecided.

## Core concept

A competitive game with a shared survival requirement and a possible hidden Anarchist, about preventing civilization's collapse while managing rapid change from approximately 2020 into an uncertain future. The first prototype will be a digital PC board game. The final format remains open, with physical and digital feasibility retained for as long as practical. Terraforming Mars is a reference point, but the game should be much lighter: 2–5 players, at most 90 minutes per session.

Players represent visible real nations with slightly different starting conditions, comparable in role to Terraforming Mars corporations. Nations are separate from secret goals. The exact nations and starting advantages are undecided.

Players decide national and global responses to technological, social, economic, and environmental breakthroughs and crises. Subject decks mix event types and provide two face-up options with immediate refill. A turn can select an event, pay to resolve a crisis (including another nation's), or pass. A passing player may discard one displayed non-capstone event, even when passing voluntarily. Capstones cannot be discarded this way. National decisions belong to the acting player. World decisions use the ballot appropriate to the card; World Projects and Policies have one effect and are voted into play or discarded without effect.

There is one voting system for ordinary world decisions and tier reviews. Each event explicitly names its eligible voting aspects, independently of its deck subject. National cards may supply multiple aspects and multiple copies of one aspect: Technology ×1 and Economy ×2 contribute three votes when both are eligible. Each nation has a minimum strength of one (a floor, not an additional vote). Yes, No, A, B and Abstain are valid as appropriate to the event. Players reveal simultaneously and declare strength; the initiating player counts and breaks ties. All matching national aspects count in tier reviews, including both surviving and outgoing cards. Voting strength is fully reusable and never spent. Review initiator assignment remains open.

All five tracks have shared world values. Every nation now has publicly visible effective collapse limits alongside EVERY world scale, with starting positions changed by Events. This replaces the earlier Employment-only limit rule and the proposed use of personal offsets for communicating collapse. National scale effects are adjustments for the current player; they do not directly change the shared value merely because that player selects them. Exact effect notation and scoring inputs after this change still need definition.

Color-coded national markers communicate limits directly. Policies/projects such as UBI can increase a nation's tolerance of falling Employment without creating jobs. One nation collapsing causes the whole world to collapse. Climate remains dangerous at both cold and hot extremes; whether each nation has independently movable lower and upper Climate markers needs confirmation. Exact threshold comparisons, permitted ranges, universal physical bounds and stacking remain open. Do not retain a second hidden personal-offset calculation that duplicates the visible limit without an explicit rule.

The original tracks were Unemployment, Happiness, Wealth, Energy, and Climate, on an approximately 1–12 scale. Employment replaces Unemployment and specifically measures how many people make ends meet by selling skills and services, excluding reliance on welfare. Livelihoods was rejected as vague. Welfare replaces Happiness and encompasses both people's overall well-being (health, security and quality of life) and public support/services. This is one combined scale, not two added tracks. Prosperity and Energy Security remain suggested names, not confirmed replacements for Wealth and Energy.

Climate is confirmed as double-edged, running from dangerous cold to dangerous heat, with a safe middle. Nuclear winter is an accepted possible fictional cause of cooling. The other tracks are intended to be dangerous at low values; thresholds and policy exceptions remain open.

Low world Wealth reduces world Welfare through an ongoing penalty; it is not multiplied once per nation. Other penalty links and thresholds remain open. Crises activate immediately when REVEALED, without waiting for selection. A National crisis automatically belongs to the revealing player; World crises enter the world area. The crisis leaves the display and refill continues until there are two non-crisis options or the deck is empty. Several crises can therefore enter play during one refill. Setup assignment when there is no revealing player, and how A/B crisis text interacts with automatic activation, remain open.

Paying to resolve a crisis replaces taking a new event that turn. Players may resolve someone else's crisis, and the helping player pays. Removal stops ongoing penalties; past damage remains. The exact resource/limit paid, amounts, affected-player consent and matching-breakthrough remedies remain open.

Each player takes a turn (new event, crisis remedy or pass). After all have acted, ongoing track penalties and crises apply once, then collapse is checked before normal completion. The starting player rotates. All normal endings wait until round end so everyone receives the same number of turns. Exact penalty ordering and newly crossed thresholds during upkeep remain open. A nation's collapse causing worldwide defeat is separate from repeated upkeep penalties.

The earlier halfway protection proposal is superseded: later collapse should emerge from balancing scale sizes, steps and stronger effects on later cards, not a rule disabling early defeat. Early collapse is therefore not explicitly prohibited; the balance target is for collapse to be likely only in later stages. Subject decks progress independently through ordered tiers, with a transition triggered by revealing the first card of a new tier. See tier progression below.

Secret goals are final-board scoring equations, not pass/fail objectives. Highest VP wins if civilization survives. Ordinary goals may reward values close to collapse, deliberately making non-Anarchists take risks. The Anarchist wins only through destabilization. Choosing World events intentionally forgoes national voting/prerequisite growth in exchange for changing the world and pursuing one's goal; no extra initiator reward is currently required. If the world collapses without an Anarchist, the original collective-loss rule implies nobody wins. Ties and exact scoring inputs remain open.

The Anarchist is not guaranteed to be present. Public nations are separate from a proposed ten-card secret goal pool containing nine scoring goals and one Anarchist: each player takes two, keeps one, and secretly discards the other. The Anarchist controls an ordinary nation and receives no special sabotage powers or special treatment for now. Cards available to ordinary players may hurt others. Revisit special powers only if playtesting demonstrates a need.

Each event deck ends with one capstone project. It uses the normal project action and can have special rules. Building any capstone or exhausting three or more draw decks schedules the end at ROUND END, even with displayed cards remaining. All players finish their turns; collapse takes precedence over either normal ending. Exact capstone requirements and special rules remain open.

## Recommendations awaiting decisions

- Candidate crisis implementation: print a costly resolution condition on each ongoing crisis and allow matching breakthroughs as an alternative. Costs, eligibility and matching remain open; spending a turn, helping other nations and preserving past damage are confirmed.
- Review votes count all matching national aspects, including outgoing cards, and are reusable. Finalize review order, initiator assignment and the handling of multiple tier transitions revealed in one crisis-refill chain.
- Expiration uses absolute tiers of one or more subjects. Specify whether multiple conditions use any/all logic and how removed cards' ongoing effects/limit adjustments are handled.
- Retain variable draw pace as an open pacing consideration: crisis-resolution turns slow tier progression and the three-deck exhaustion ending.

## Proposed card system

- Scope: National or World.
- Event types: Crisis (mostly negative), Breakthrough (mostly positive), Project, Policy.
- Aspects include Technology, Governance, Ecology, Industry, Economy, and Social; the taxonomy is open.
- Resolutions may carry different aspects from the event itself.
- CONFIRMED: the chosen resolution supplies persistent aspects, potentially multiple copies of multiple aspects. Unresolved A/B events are landscape; rotate left 90 degrees for A or right 90 degrees for B. In tableaus, show the selected resolution, aspects, ongoing effects, scale/limit adjustments and expiry; hide the ineffective resolution. Single-effect Projects and Policies do not need artificial A/B outcomes.
- CONFIRMED: resolved national events remain in a player's tableau and contribute aspects to voting and prerequisites. Active crises also contribute aspects; resolving/removing one removes its aspects.
- CONFIRMED: cards specify absolute expiry tiers of one or more subjects; some Tier 1 policies may remain to game end.
- Projects and Policies have one effect. World versions are voted into play or discarded without effect. Prerequisites count shared World cards plus YOUR National cards, not other nations' national cards. Whether requirements are checked only on construction or maintained thereafter remains open.
- Policies alter rules. National policies may steal or destroy opponents' cards, or give them crises, to limit snowballing.
- A breakthrough can potentially discard a crisis of the corresponding type; matching and effect reversal are undecided.
- CONFIRMED: crises can have ongoing effects and costly remedies; resolving them stops ongoing penalties while past damage remains.

## Tier progression, aging and policy reviews

“Tier” replaces Age/epoch. Subject decks advance independently through ordered tiers. Revealing the first new-tier card triggers the transition, but the currently selected event finishes resolving BEFORE review and discard. Technology can advance while Climate remains behind. Previously displayed older-tier cards still need an explicit handling rule.

Cards expire at printed absolute tiers of one or more subjects; changing a card's chosen aspect does not automatically change its clock. A Technology card resolved as Governance still follows Technology unless its printed expiry specifies another subject. Some cards can persist until game end. Multiple expiry conditions need any/all semantics.

Outgoing cards participate alongside all surviving matching national cards in every review vote, before final discard. They leave no lasting influence after removal. Votes are reusable. Each player nominates one World event of the ADVANCING subject; each event can be nominated only once in that review. Review order, optional nomination and initiator assignment remain open.

Expiration preserves historical world-track changes. Changing a World event's resolution instead UNDOES the previous proposal and applies the new one. For uncomplicated additive effects this corresponds to subtracting A and adding B. Reversal of capped values, removed targets, costs and non-numeric effects still needs rules; do not conflate revision with expiration.

Still open: any/all expiry conditions; review order and optional nomination; review tie-breaker assignment; outgoing ongoing-effect timing; setup/new-tier ordering; multiple transitions in one refill chain; and non-additive reversal accounting.

## Fiction and themes

- AI and robots replace most jobs; unemployment rises. Basic income and public services can soften the effect on happiness. Demand for specialist education falls.
- Corporate power can develop into feudal dependence. Information becomes cheap and privacy erodes.
- Drone and robot proxy wars lower the barriers to expansionism.
- Resource depletion pushes humanity into space exploration and extraction.
- Repeated technological disruption forces people to adapt to a world that increasingly does not need their labor.
- Climate change drives disasters, famine, extinctions, displacement, and ecosystem destruction.
- Algorithms exploit human behavior and deepen consumerism.
- Medical progress could extend lifespan to 150 years, while personalized entertainment, lifelike robots, and escapism contribute to loneliness and rising suicide rates.
- Raising children becomes unaffordable for many without public support. Population initially rises with longevity, then falls as reproduction declines.

The game's premise is societal adaptation: AI/digital automation, increasing robotics and climate change remain pressures that policy mitigates rather than simply removes. A high-employment future is not the intended baseline at present. This is a fictional design premise, not a claim that near-total unemployment is an established forecast. Research will compare observed trends, conditional forecasts and disagreements before proposing event sequences. Other stronger claims in the original brief, such as 150-year lifespans, require separate evidence or explicit speculative labeling. See research-plan.md.

Writing should be clear enough for teenagers and sound like public-policy discussion: restrained, sometimes clinical, never gratuitously graphic. A sensitive event could be titled “Mental-health emergency” with factual body text such as “Suicide deaths are rising. Access to care has not kept pace.” This is a proposed editorial example, not a forecast or a finished card.
