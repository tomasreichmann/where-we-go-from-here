# Future-scenario research plan

## Purpose and current status

Research plausible trajectories for AI, digital work, robotics and climate to inform this game's event decks and policy choices. The creative premise is **societal adaptation to persistent pressures**. Research should test timing, mechanisms and consequences, including evidence that complicates the premise, rather than seek only confirmation that most jobs disappear.

This is a research plan. Initial primary-source discovery and age/tone guidance were checked on 2026-09-24; the full forecast synthesis has not been performed. No research automation or separate task has been scheduled.

The static prototype may use clearly labeled provisional cards while research proceeds later. Findings should refine content without pretending that game track values are calibrated economic or climate forecasts.

## Research questions and scope

| Workstream | Questions | Intended game-design output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI and digital employment | Which tasks are technically exposed, which are actually automated, and how quickly are firms adopting systems? What differs by occupation/country? What could offset displacement? | Credible sequence of administrative, professional and institutional disruptions; distinguish capability breakthroughs from adoption and labor outcomes. |
| Physical robotics | Separate industrial, logistics, service, medical and domestic robots. What deployment, cost, dexterity, reliability and infrastructure barriers constrain adoption? | Event prompts with plausible prerequisites, lags, resource demands and sector-specific consequences. |
| Climate risks | What changes are already observed or committed, what depends on future emissions, and where are adaptation limits? Compare heat, food/water risks, disasters and displacement. | Persistent world pressures and policies that reduce vulnerability; avoid implying that adaptation alone stops warming or that mitigation cannot change future severity. |
| Energy and resources | How do AI compute, electrification and robotics affect demand? What constrains grids, storage, generation and critical-material supply? | Tradeoffs connecting Energy, Wealth, Climate and deployment pace; infrastructure project chains. |
| Social and fiscal adaptation | What evidence supports income support, universal services, work sharing, education changes, public investment and different ownership models? Who gains/loses, how are they funded, and what is uncertain? | Meaningful policies that move national limits while leaving some underlying world pressure intact. |

Keep broader original themes—150-year lifespans, demographic collapse, off-world extraction and corporate feudalism—as a separately labeled speculative backlog unless the user expands the research. Do not present them as findings of this narrower study.

## Evidence and forecasting method

1. Establish observed changes since the game's 2020 starting point. Record the observation year separately from publication date.
2. At execution time, check for newer editions and revisions of every source below; the seed list is not a claim to contain the latest available study.
3. Extract near-term projections through roughly 2030 and medium-term scenarios through 2040–2050. Treat longer horizons separately, especially for climate; do not force the same certainty onto AI and climate timescales.
4. Separate observations, exposure/capability estimates, deployment forecasts, conditional scenarios, expert speculation and fictional extrapolation.
5. Record geography, sectors, definitions, assumptions, ranges, uncertainty/confidence and policy dependencies. Do not compare a task-exposure percentage with an unemployment forecast as if they measured the same thing.
6. For consequential contested claims, compare at least two independent primary sources where available. Identify shared datasets rather than count repeated reporting as independent confirmation.
7. Include disagreement and constraints. A low-employment fictional scenario may remain the chosen setting even when it is not the central estimate of the evidence.
8. Produce three distinguishable trajectories: managed transition, uneven adaptation and accelerating disruption. They are conditional scenarios, not equally probable predictions unless sources justify probabilities.
9. Translate evidence into event prompts and policy tradeoffs. Clearly mark the step from source evidence to game inference; do not assert real forecasts justify exact +1/−2 balance values.

Do not assign calendar years rigidly to every tier yet. Independent subject tiers can represent capability/adoption stages whose relative pacing is informed by research.

## Primary-source starting points

The links below were located or opened during planning. Full methodology review is part of the later research, not already completed.

- [ILO: Generative AI and Jobs — A Refined Global Index of Occupational Exposure (2025)](https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-refined-global-index-occupational-exposure). Useful for task/occupation exposure and country/sector distinctions. The source expects transformation to be more common than complete replacement; it does not justify treating all exposed jobs as lost jobs.
- [ILO: Artificial intelligence adoption and its impact on jobs (2025)](https://www.ilo.org/publications/artificial-intelligence-adoption-and-its-impact-jobs). Candidate source for the gap between exposure, adoption and realized employment effects.
- [IFR: World Robotics — Service Robots](https://ifr.org/wr-service-robots) and [2025 industrial-robot executive summary](https://ifr.org/img/worldrobotics/Executive_Summary_WR_2025_Industrial_Robots.pdf). Starting points for sector definitions and deployment evidence. Verify the current edition, underlying coverage and sample limits; distinguish industry measurement from promotional forecasts.
- [IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report: Summary for Policymakers](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/summary-for-policymakers/). Baseline for physical climate risks, mitigation and adaptation; retain the source's conditionality and confidence statements.
- [IPCC Working Group II: headline findings](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/resources/spm-headline-statements/). Starting point for vulnerability and limits of adaptation; follow claims into the report's underlying analysis.
- [IEA: Energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai). Starting point for energy demand, supply, infrastructure and scenario assumptions around AI.

During execution, expand selectively to OECD/IMF employment and fiscal research, national statistical data and relevant original studies. Use secondary summaries to locate primary sources, not as the final basis for quantitative predictions. Avoid relying on a single vendor's roadmap for robot capability or AGI timing.

## Proposed deliverables

- An evidence register with claim ID, topic, source URL, date, data period, horizon, geography, claim type, assumptions, confidence/uncertainty and limitations.
- A concise scenario brief describing persistent pressures, plausible speeds of change, policy-sensitive outcomes and major disagreements.
- A matrix of policy levers and tradeoffs mapped to the game's world values and national limits, keeping real-world effects separate from game balance.
- Approximately 12–16 researched event briefs across AI/work, robotics, climate and energy/adaptation. Each contains a content prompt, supporting claim IDs, uncertainty note, candidate responses and illustration prompt. This target is a research scope suggestion, not a full-deck commitment.
- A speculative-backlog list for unsupported or distant-future ideas, so imaginative content can remain without masquerading as an established prediction.

Store source links and rationale in development annotations. Player-facing card prose should remain short and understandable. In the eventual app, the rules' development toggle exposes the research notes; production hides those annotations.

## Audience, wording and visual treatment

Use **16+ as a provisional editorial target**, under the user's stated flexibility. This is a creative scope choice, not an assigned PEGI/ESRB classification. The finished game's actual depictions and context will determine its rating; the [PEGI label guide](https://pegi.info/what-do-the-labels-mean) and [ESRB guide](https://www.esrb.org/ratings-guide/) describe different systems and categories.

The original 14+ goal remains reflected in readable language and approachable mechanics. Do not add mature material simply because the working audience target is higher. Bright solarpunk visuals can coexist with serious policy dilemmas.

User-approved boundaries: dark themes and implied disorder/crime are acceptable; gore and explicit violence are not. Write in straightforward public-policy language. Use a softer event title where useful, but avoid euphemisms so obscure that teenagers cannot understand the issue.

Proposed example: **Mental-health emergency** — “Suicide deaths are rising. Access to care has not kept pace.” This is a wording example, not a research finding or finished event. Policy responses can address care, social connection and support without depicting acts or methods. Do not portray suicide as a simple inevitable consequence of one technology or as a beneficial population/economic outcome.

The [WHO communication resource (2023)](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240076846) is a relevant editorial reference for responsible public communication and emphasis on prevention/recovery. It is not a game-rating rulebook. Its detailed recommendations should be consulted when writing the actual sensitive cards. Do not invent a rule that merely mentioning suicide automatically requires a particular age rating.

## Completion criteria for the later research

- Major quantitative claims link to directly supporting primary sources and distinguish exposure, adoption, displacement and net employment outcomes.
- Publication and observation dates are visible; superseded evidence is marked.
- Climate adaptation and emissions mitigation remain distinct policy levers with different effects.
- Scenario uncertainty and disagreements survive the translation into event briefs.
- Fictional extrapolation, national starting assumptions and illustrative game numbers are labeled explicitly.
- Resulting event briefs explore adaptation tradeoffs and do not turn one favored forecast into an unquestionable future.
